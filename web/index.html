Your data stays on this device

Everything you do in Mind’s Eye Lab is saved only on this iPhone. Your answers, scores and task results are never uploaded, no one else can see them, and there are no accounts and no tracking. You can erase everything from the Results screen whenever you like.

About this app

Mind’s Eye Lab is a free, non-commercial app from the Imagination and Modal Cognition Lab at Duke University. It is an educational self-assessment and training tool. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose any condition, including aphantasia. A future version may offer optional participation in imagery research; that would always be opt-in and fully described before anything is collected.

What this can and can't tell you

Aphantasia is identified by self-report. Results are shown under two conventions: the common bands (16–32 aphantasia, 33–47 low, 48–74 typical, 75–80 hyperphantasia) and a stricter one in which only 16 counts as core aphantasia. Objective tasks cannot classify you; what differs across the spectrum is usually strategy, not score. Training improves spatial skill and your insight into your own imagery; no study has shown it makes images more vivid. This is a screening tool, not a clinical diagnosis.

Training games

Short daily sessions; each game gets harder as you clear levels. Training is known to improve spatial skill, span and insight into your own imagery; it has not been shown to make images more vivid.

Athletes: pre-game visualization

Build a personal script for your sport, then run a guided 6-minute session: warm-up, the venue, your key skill, a setback and recovery, and the finish. Based on the PETTLEP model used in sport psychology.

Guided practices
